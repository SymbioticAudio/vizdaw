# VizDAW 5f-4 — Kontrol S-Series MK3 (DAW mode and plug-in pages) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A Kontrol S-Series MK3 keyboard (S49, S61, S88; firmware 2.0 or later) drives VizDAW over its "DAW" MIDI port as a fourth surface kind. Its transport buttons run the transport; its Count-in and Automation buttons toggle VizDAW's record count-in and Suspend Automation; its tempo control and Tap Tempo change VizDAW's tempo at the playhead, one undo step per gesture (owner, 2026-11-03). Its eight knobs, track buttons and 4-D encoder ride and switch an 8-track bank of the mixer, with track and bank navigation; its screen shows each track's name, type, colour, volume and pan text, mute, solo, arm, selection and meters, and VizDAW's tempo. In Plug-in mode it shows VizDAW's own parameter pages for any plug-in on the selected track (the chain, eight parameter names and values per page, the page, the preset name), its knobs move those parameters, and its preset buttons step the plug-in's own presets. Its "Main" port stays an ordinary keyboard for the lanes. A keyboard switched out of DAW mode is pulled back by the hello VizDAW repeats every 2 s (owner). A `surfaces.json` that lists a kind this build does not know keeps that entry and loads the rest (controller ruling K-0).

**Architecture:**
- **`core-surface::kontrol`** is a new pure module: the wire codec (the NI SysEx frame, display text, tempo, relative values, port matching, meter scale, track types and colours) and the `Kontrol` state machine behind the existing `Surface` trait (handshake, input, feedback with a send-only-what-changed cache, goodbye). It runs and is tested on Linux.
- **The shared types grow by what the Kontrol shows and asks:** `TrackKind` and `StripView.kind`; `MixerView.tempo`, `.soloing`, `.count_in`, `.automation_suspended` and `.page` (a `PluginPageView`); `SurfaceAction::PluginSlot`, `PluginPage`, `Preset`, `Tempo { bpm, new_step }` and `CountIn`; trait defaults `shows_page`, `goodbye` and `take_problem`.
- **`core-ffi::surfaces`** adds the kind (`SurfaceKindRow::KontrolMk3`, its own bank of 8, position 0), goodbye on removal (after its holds are answered) and on `shutdown`, the unit's problem lines, its meters counted, the plug-in page when a Kontrol in Plug-in mode is due, `kontrol_ports()` (the ports whose names say KONTROL, MK3 and DAW), and a loader that keeps entries of unknown kinds verbatim (C1). `SurfaceEventRow` gains `CountIn`.
- **`Session`** fills the new view fields, keeps each document's page focus (which plug-in of the selected strip's chain and which page), and applies the new actions: the page and preset actions, the keyboard's tempo (one undo step per gesture through `History::amend`) and the count-in event. A new export, `set_surface_count_in`, tells it the app's count-in for the light. A preset step goes through two new `PluginInstance` methods: `presets` and `load_preset`. AU implements them with its factory presets, and its load notes the parameter values it causes as the host's own writes (I2). CLAP keeps the defaults (none).
- **The app:** Settings › Surfaces offers "Kontrol S-Series MK3 (DAW mode)", picks the DAW port by itself, shows each row's problem line and the trademark notice. The surface relay toggles the record count-in on `CountIn`, and the document tells the core its count-in. The channel's `surface.add` takes `type: "kontrol_mk3"`. Also an e2e, README 189–198 and backlog rows.

**Tech Stack:** Rust 2024 (`core-surface`, `core-ffi` with UniFFI 0.32.2, `core-host`, `platform-macos`, `core-control`, `vizctl`), Swift 6 / SwiftPM (Swift Testing), macOS 14+.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-recording-audio-design.md`. The writer's commit adds §8.4 "Komplete Kontrol MK3" and the §13 row "5f-4"; the revision's commit updates §8.3 and §8.4 with the owner's answers of 2026-11-03 and the pre-flight's rulings. - **Protocol values used verbatim (the byte tables of Rulings 4, 5, 6 and 8):**
  - The SysEx header is `F0 00 21 09 00 00 44 43 01 00`. A message is `header, cmd, value, index, info…, F7`.
  - All CCs are on MIDI channel 16 (status `BF`).
  - The host's hello is `BF 01 04`; the keyboard answers `BF 01 <version>`. Goodbye is `BF 02 00`.
  - The tempo is "5 × 7-bit little-endian = length of a quarter note in units of 10 ns", that is `round(6e9 / bpm)`, in both directions (SysEx 0x19, v4).

  - Relative values: "0–63 positive, 64–127 negative (two's complement on 7 bits)".
  - Track types: "0 empty, 1 generic, 2 MIDI, 3 audio, 4 group, 5 return, 6 master".
  - The chain is "NUL-separated plug-in names, double-NUL terminated".
  - The VU is "value 2, index 0, info = 16 bytes (L/R for 8 tracks, 0–127)".
  - The colour is `"#RRGGBB"` text.

## Review Focus

These are the six failure modes a user is most likely to hit, most likely first. The spec implies them but does not spell them out. Each is pinned by named tests in its task.

1. **The keyboard that never comes online, or goes and does not come back.** The DAW port is picked wrong (the "Main" or "MIDI 2.0" port), the keyboard runs old firmware and answers version 3, it is switched on after VizDAW, the user switches it out of DAW mode, or a newer VizDAW wrote a `surfaces.json` kind this build does not know.
   - Expected:
     - Settings picks the port whose name has KONTROL, MK3 and DAW.
     - A version below 4 shows a problem line naming the version and "firmware 2.0", in `state.get surfaces` **and under the row in Settings › Surfaces** (I4), and the unit stays "Connected · not answering". A version below 4 arriving while online takes it offline the same way (M13).
     - The hello is repeated every 2 s until an answer comes.
     - A keyboard goodbye (or switching it out of DAW mode, if it says so) releases what it held; VizDAW keeps asking every 2 s, so the keyboard is **pulled back into DAW mode** (owner, 2026-11-03), and the answer brings it back with its name and a **full** refresh: every message of the first feedback again, not only what changed.
     - An entry of an unknown kind is kept, named in a problem line, and the MCU and HUI entries still load; nothing is set aside (C1).
   - Pinned by `daw_ports_are_found_by_kontrol_mk3_and_daw`, `a_wrong_version_is_a_problem_and_keeps_it_offline`, `a_lower_version_while_online_takes_it_offline`, `hello_is_repeated_every_2_s_until_answered`, `the_keyboards_goodbye_takes_it_offline_until_it_answers_again`, `a_wrong_protocol_version_is_named_in_the_problems`, `kontrol_ports_lists_only_daw_ports` and `a_surfaces_file_with_an_unknown_kind_keeps_the_rest` (Task 1); `a_goodbye_from_the_keyboard_releases_its_knobs` (Task 2); `a_keyboard_pulled_back_into_daw_mode_gets_everything_again` (Task 3); `a_keyboard_switched_out_of_daw_mode_is_pulled_back_with_a_full_refresh` (Task 4); `aKontrolDraftPicksTheKeyboardsDawPorts`, `aKontrolDraftWithoutAKeyboardSaysSo` and `aKontrolWithOldFirmwareSaysWhy` (Task 6).
2. **Keys leaking out, or knobs leaking in.** The lane is armed on All MIDI Inputs and the user plays the keys and turns the knobs.
   - Expected: the keys (Main port) reach the engine and record and play thru as before, and the surface hears none of them. Nothing from the DAW port reaches a lane: no knob CC, no transport CC, no SysEx.
   - Pinned by `the_daw_port_is_the_surfaces_and_the_main_port_the_lanes` (Task 1), which holds the engine (`play`), so its Main-port assertion fails if the surface claimed Main or the keys went nowhere (M3).
3. **A knob ride that never ends or ends twice.** A volume knob is turned and let go. While it is held, the USB cable is pulled, the surface is removed, the window loses focus, or the keyboard says goodbye.
   - Expected: the ride is one "Set Fader" step, released 500 ms after the last turn and stamped at the last value. Every way of losing the unit releases it exactly once. On removal the holds are answered **before** the goodbye is sent, and the goodbye neither forgets nor answers holds (I1).
   - Pinned by `volume_and_pan_knobs_ride_the_shown_strips_and_release_after_idle`, `a_goodbye_from_the_keyboard_releases_its_knobs` and `release_all_and_release_holds_answer_the_knobs_it_holds` (Task 2), and `a_volume_knob_ride_is_one_set_fader_step` and `removing_a_kontrol_while_a_knob_is_held_releases_it_once` (Task 4). The 5f-1 tests of the shared paths still run.
4. **The screen showing the wrong thing.** The user banks past the last strip, selects off-screen, solos a track in another bank, changes the selection while in Plug-in mode, turns the keyboard's own tempo control, or focuses a plug-in with thousands of parameters.
   - Expected:
     - Slots past the last strip are empty.
     - Track navigation moves the Kontrol's own bank to the selection, and the MCU group's bank does not move.
     - Muted-by-solo lights for every unsoloed track when any track is soloed.
     - The navigation lights say where one can go.
     - Changing the selected strip starts its plug-in view at the chain's first plug-in, page 1. The mode switch shows the page at once (M15).
     - After the keyboard sends a tempo, the next feedback sends VizDAW's tempo again (the one it now plays, or the old one when the change was refused); the keyboard's echo of VizDAW's own tempo is not answered, so they never ping-pong.
     - More than 127 pages (over 1016 parameters) never breaks a SysEx frame: page, count and slot are sent as at most 127 (I3).
   - Pinned by `track_navigation_selects_the_next_strip_and_the_bank_follows` (Task 2), `a_bank_shows_the_next_eight_strips`, `the_navigation_lights_say_where_one_can_go`, `mute_solo_arm_select_and_muted_by_solo_light`, `the_tempo_is_sent_again_after_the_keyboards_own` and `more_than_127_pages_stay_seven_bit` (Task 3), `sysex_keeps_value_and_index_seven_bit` (Task 1), and `track_navigation_moves_the_kontrols_own_bank_with_the_selection`, `the_kontrol_sees_kinds_colours_tempo_and_solo`, `plugin_mode_pages_through_the_chain_and_a_knob_moves_the_parameter` and `the_plugin_page_starts_over_when_the_selection_changes` (Task 4).
5. **A button that does more than it says.** A preset step, a tempo turn, a few taps, Count-in or Automation pressed on the keyboard.
   - Expected:
     - A preset step on an AU that notifies its parameters makes no "Set …" undo step and, while a strip writes automation, no lane per parameter: the notices the load causes are the host's own writes (I2). The project reads Edited, and a save keeps the preset.
     - A tempo turn (a run of the keyboard's tempo messages less than 500 ms apart) is one "Set Tempo" step; a tap series is one step; the tempo changes at the playhead (the tempo point in effect there); while recording both are refused and counted, and the tempo does not move (owner, 2026-11-03).
     - Count-in toggles the document's record count-in (off ↔ its last bar count) and Automation toggles Suspend Automation; their lights follow VizDAW's state, including changes made in VizDAW (owner).
   - Pinned by `a_preset_load_is_not_a_gesture` and `a_preset_load_refreshes_the_parameters_and_is_no_undo_step` (Task 5); `the_keyboards_tempo_sets_vizdaws_in_runs` and `tap_tempo_sets_the_tempo_from_the_taps_intervals` (Task 2); `transport_lights_follow_play_record_loop_count_in_and_suspend` (Task 3); `the_keyboards_tempo_and_tap_change_vizdaws_one_step_per_gesture`, `the_keyboards_tempo_is_refused_while_recording`, `count_in_and_automation_reach_the_app_and_light_with_its_state`, `aCountInPressTogglesTheRecordCountIn` and `idleTellsTheSurfacesTheCountIn` (Task 7).
6. **A flood or a stall.** The keyboard gets every message again every 20 ms, or a long or non-ASCII track name, a fast meter, or garbage from the port stalls it.
   - Expected:
     - Only what changed is sent.
     - Text is printable ASCII and at most 32 bytes; a volume text is at most 8 characters ("-inf dB" below −99.9 dB, M9).
     - Meters go as one 16-byte message, are counted rather than logged, and are not resent while unchanged.
     - The preset list is not asked of the plug-in on every feedback (M7).
     - Garbage is counted and never panics.
   - Pinned by `only_what_changed_is_sent_again`, `volume_and_pan_read_as_text` and `meters_are_one_message_of_sixteen_levels` (Task 3), `display_text_is_printable_ascii_and_at_most_32_bytes` (Task 1), `kontrol_meters_are_counted_not_listed` (Task 4) and `hostile_kontrol_bytes_are_ignored_and_counted` (Task 2).

1. **Ruling (scope and order).**
   - **What:** One plan, 8 tasks.
     - Phase 1 (owner): handshake and identification, transport (with Count-in, Automation and Tap, owner 2026-11-03), an 8-track mixer with banking and navigation, tempo both ways, track colours, meters.
     - Phase 2 (owner): Plug-in mode with VizDAW's own pages for any plug-in, and preset previous/next.

   - **Why:**
     - Phase 2 reuses phase 1's state machine, cache and registry wiring.
     - Phase 1 alone is about 4 tasks, so two plans would duplicate the header and the final review.
   - **Cost if wrong:** one larger final review.
2. **Ruling (the kind and its bank).**
   - **What:**
     - `SurfaceKind::KontrolMk3` (serde `"kontrol_mk3"`, `SurfaceKindRow::KontrolMk3`, channel `type: "kontrol_mk3"`) is configured on the keyboard's DAW port for input and output.
     - Each Kontrol is a bank group of its own (`Group::Kontrol(SurfaceId)`): 8 strips from its own offset, as a HUI is. Its position must be 0 (refused otherwise: "a Kontrol S-Series MK3 keyboard is a bank of its own: `position` must be 0").
     - It counts toward the 16 surfaces. Several Kontrols are allowed.
     - The Main port is never configured, so the 5f-1 claims leave it to the lanes (owner).
   - **Why:**
     - The keyboard shows 8 tracks and has its own bank buttons.
     - Joining the MCU group would move the MCU's faders whenever the keyboard banks.
   - **Cost if wrong:** a group change in `Unit::group`.
3. **Ruling (finding the port; owner: "match robustly").**
   - **What:**
     - `kontrol::is_daw_port(name)` splits the name, upper-cased, at every character that is not a letter or digit. It is true when the words include `KONTROL`, `MK3` and `DAW`.
     - That matches `KONTROL S88 MK3 DAW` and `Native Instruments - KONTROL S61 MK3 - DAW`. It does not match `… Main`, `… MIDI 2.0`, the MK2's `Komplete Kontrol DAW - 1` or the Windows/Linux names.
     - `kontrol_ports()` answers the transport's sources and destinations that match, in the transport's order.
     - Settings fills both pickers with the first match when the type becomes the Kontrol, and the user can pick any port by hand.
   - **Why:**
     - The research shows two macOS spellings, endpoint versus "manufacturer - device - entity".
     - The owner confirms the names in alpha testing (README 189 asks for them).
   - **Cost if wrong:** one function and its test, or a hand pick.
4. **Ruling (handshake, identity, goodbye).**
   - **What:**
     - `hello()` is `BF 01 04` (protocol version 4).
     - The keyboard's answer `BF 01 v` puts the unit online when `v ≥ 4` (it speaks 4).
     - When `v < 4` the unit stays offline. Once per answer it reports the problem "the keyboard answered protocol version {v}; VizDAW needs version 4 (keyboard firmware 2.0 or later)". The registry prefixes it with "surface {id}: " and adds it to `state.get surfaces` problems, and Settings › Surfaces shows it under the unit's row (Ruling 10, I4).
     - A version below 4 arriving **while online** takes the unit offline as the keyboard's goodbye does (its releases answered), and reports the same problem once (pre-flight M13).
     - Until an answer of 4 or more, every other message is ignored and counted, and feedback sends nothing except the hello again `HELLO_EVERY` = 2 s after the last one.
     - Every accepted answer clears the send cache: the next feedback starts with IDENTITY `sysex(0x07, 1, 0, b"Generic")` (DAW version 1.0, name "Generic"), followed by the **full** state, whatever was sent before. A refresh (bank change, reconnect) resends the state but not the identity.
     - The keyboard's `BF 02 xx` takes the unit offline. It answers the releases of what the unit held, through the shared `Unit::set_online`, and restarts the hello timer: the hello goes again `HELLO_EVERY` after the goodbye, and every 2 s after that until an answer.
     - **Pulled back (owner, 2026-11-03).** If the user switches the keyboard out of DAW mode and it says goodbye, the repeated hello pulls it back into DAW mode, and its answer gives it the identity and the full state. VizDAW does not leave it out until a replug. Whether the keyboard says goodbye when switched, and whether it takes a hello outside DAW mode, are README 198 checks.
     - `goodbye()` is TRACK_AVAILABLE 0 for slots 0–7, then `BF 02 00`; afterwards the unit is offline. It returns these bytes whether or not the unit is online and **neither forgets nor answers holds**: whoever lets the unit go answers them first with `release_all` (pre-flight I1).
     - `Registry::remove` takes the unit's `release_all()` and owes it to the focused session, then sends `goodbye()` while the unit is still in `units` (when it is connected and was online), then drops it.
     - `surfaces::goodbye_all()`, called from `shutdown()` when the app quits, sends every connected, online unit's `goodbye()`, then makes the registry **quit**: the CoreMIDI transport is dropped, which joins its sender after its last drain (`MidiSystem::drop`, `midi.rs:496–501`), so the goodbye leaves the process before it exits (pre-flight M2); `ports()` is not opened again and nothing more is sent (no hello). Under `test-hooks` the fake transport is kept, so `debug_sent` reads what was sent.
     - Closing the focused document blanks the unit as 5f-1 does: an empty view, so every slot reads empty.
   - **Why:**
     - DrivenByMoss flushes state only after the version reply.
     - The identity name picks the plug-in view's background. An unknown name reportedly gives an unreadable one.
     - Sending another DAW's name would misrepresent VizDAW. "Generic" names no product and is the neutral choice DrivenByMoss offers.
     - Answering holds before the goodbye keeps the owner's "releases are never lost" on removal: the goodbye's offline mark cannot drop an idle hold nobody answered.
     - Joining the CoreMIDI sender at quit is the only way the last bytes leave: `send` only queues into a ring a 1 ms thread drains.
   - **Cost if wrong:** the background is unreadable. That is one constant (`IDENTITY_NAME`), and README 198 checks it; "VizDAW" is the fallback. A keyboard that does not say goodbye when switched out of DAW mode is not pulled back (VizDAW thinks it is still online); README 198 asks, and a keep-alive would be the fix (backlog).
5. **Ruling (what the controls do).**
   - **What:** The keyboard sends these, on channel 16 unless they are SysEx.
     - **Transport** (act on a value above 0; a 0 is a release and does nothing):
       - `10` Play → `Command("transport.play")`, which only starts (owner);
       - `11` Restart → `Command("transport.return_to_start")`, then `Command("transport.play")`;
       - `12` Record → `transport.record`; `14` Stop → `transport.stop`; `16` Loop → `transport.loop`;
       - `20` Undo → `edit.undo`; `21` Redo → `edit.redo`.
       - `13` Count-in → `CountIn` (owner, 2026-11-03): the session queues `SurfaceEventRow::CountIn`, and the app toggles the document's record count-in between off and its last bar count (1 when it never had one; Ruling 16).
       - `23` Automation → `Command("automation.suspend")` (owner): the existing command, which toggles Suspend Automation when `on` is absent.
       - `18` Tap Tempo → the tapped tempo (owner; below).
       - Clear `15`, Metro `17`, Quantize `22`, Shift `04`, clip and scene navigation `32`/`33` and Move Loop `35` do nothing and are **not** counted. VizDAW has no metronome, and its quantize opens a sheet.
       - The keyboard has no Rewind or Fast Forward. Move Transport `34` (relative) is `Jog(n)`, a sixteenth note per step in the app (5f-1 Ruling 12).
     - **Knobs:**
       - Volume `50+k` and Pan `58+k` (k = 0–7, relative) move the fader or pan of the strip shown in slot k through `IdleTouches`: touched by the first value, released 500 ms after the last (5f-1 Ruling 2).
       - Each step moves the target by `KNOB_STEP` = 0.005 of its range, so 200 steps cover the travel.
       - A relative value of 0 or 64 is no movement and is not counted.
     - **Selected track:**
       - The 4-D encoder's `64` (volume) and `65` (pan) are relative moves of the selected strip, the first of the shown strips whose `select` is set.
       - `66`/`67` with a value above 0 press mute or solo on it, as clicks at the toggled value. A value of 0 is a release and does nothing, uncounted, as for transport (pre-flight M6).
       - With no selected strip among the shown ones, all four are ignored and counted.
     - **Track buttons (SysEx):** `42` select → `Select(strip)`; `43` mute and `44` solo → clicks at the toggled value; `45` arm → `Arm(strip)`. The *index* is the slot. A slot past 7 or an empty slot is ignored and counted.
     - **Navigation:**
       - `30` tracks (relative sign: 1–63 next, 65–127 previous) selects the next or previous strip in display order, starting from the selected one; with none selected, the first shown. When the new strip is outside the shown 8, `Channel(±d)` moves the bank just enough to show it. At either end nothing happens, uncounted.
       - `31` banks is `Bank(±1)` in Mixer mode and `PluginPage(±1)` in Plug-in mode.
       - `36` presets is `Preset(±1)` in either mode.
     - **Mode:** `05` with value 0 is Mixer, 1 is Plug-in. Other values are ignored and counted. The mode is echoed back in feedback. A mode change makes the unit due at once (Ruling 9), so the plug-in page shows without waiting for the next 20 ms tick (pre-flight M15).
     - **Plug-in (SysEx and CC):**
       - Parameter knobs `70+k` (relative) move parameter k of the shown page through `IdleTouches`, in Plug-in mode only (in Mixer mode they are ignored and counted). A knob with no parameter is ignored and counted.
       - SysEx `70` (the soft buttons, index = chain slot) → `PluginSlot(index)`; a slot past the shown chain is ignored and counted.
       - SysEx `74` (index = page) → `PluginPage(index − shown page)`. The page shown already is nothing, uncounted; a page past the count is ignored and counted. (The research lists `74` only host → keyboard; reading it from the keyboard is harmless and a README 198 check, pre-flight M14.)
     - **Tempo (owner, 2026-11-03: the keyboard's tempo control and Tap change VizDAW's tempo).**
       - SysEx `19` from the keyboard carries a tempo: `tempo_bpm` of the first five info bytes → `Tempo { bpm, new_step }`. A tempo message without five valid bytes (cut, a byte above 0x7F, a zero length) is ignored and counted.
       - `new_step` is true when the last tempo action of this unit was `TEMPO_RUN` (500 ms, the knobs' idle release) or more ago, or was a tap's: a turn of the tempo control is a run of messages, and the session makes the run one undo step (Ruling 15).
       - The keyboard's echo is nothing: a `19` whose five bytes equal the tempo VizDAW last sent this unit is uncounted and acts on nothing, so the two never ping-pong.
       - Any other `19` forgets the cached tempo, so the next feedback sends VizDAW's tempo again: the one it now plays, or the old one when the session refused the change (pre-flight I5, adapted).
       - `18` Tap with a value above 0 at `now`: a tap more than `TAP_RESET` (2 s) after the last one starts a new series and gives no tempo. Otherwise the tempo is 60 s over the mean of the series' last intervals (up to 4, that is the last 5 taps), rounded to 0.1 BPM, as `Tempo { bpm, new_step }` with `new_step` true for the first tempo of a series (its second tap). One tap, a release (value 0) and a new series' first tap act on nothing and are not counted.
     - **Ignored and counted:** any other CC on channel 16, any message on another channel (including notes), SysEx with another header, a cut frame, an unknown command, and `7F` (high-resolution deltas, which VizDAW never asks for).
   - **Why:**
     - These are DrivenByMoss's mappings, mapped onto VizDAW's existing registry commands and the 5f-1 gesture rules.
     - Not counting buttons that have no counterpart keeps `ignored` a fault counter.
     - Count-in and Automation have VizDAW counterparts (the record count-in of 5d-1, Suspend Automation of 5e-1), and the owner asked for them (pre-flight M16, Q3). Metro, Quantize and Clear have none a button can run.
     - The tempo is the owner's: "the keyboard's tempo encoder and Tap change VizDAW's tempo". Deciding the run in the unit keeps the session's rule (amend into the run's step) free of timing, and the Kontrol's clock is the surfaces clock tests drive.
   - **Cost if wrong:** one table entry each. `KNOB_STEP`, `TEMPO_RUN`, `TAP_RESET` and whether the keyboard echoes VizDAW's tempo are README 198 checks with backlog rows.
6. **Ruling (VizDAW's own plug-in pages; owner: "VizDAW's own parameter pages for any plug-in").**
   - **What:**
     - The **chain** of a strip is its plug-in instrument, when the strip is `Instrument(d)` or `InstrumentOutput(d, _)` and `d` is a plug-in instrument, followed by its inserts in processing order. Names are `PluginRef.name`.
     - Each document keeps a **page focus** (`SessionSurfaces.page: PageFocus { strip, slot, page }`). Whenever the first selected strip differs from `focus.strip`, the focus becomes that strip, slot 0, page 0.
     - The **pages** of the focused plug-in are its automatable, non-hidden parameters in the plug-in's list order (the MCU's filter), cut into pages of 8. `pages = min(127, max(1, ceil(n / 8)))`: the protocol's bytes are 7-bit, so the parameters past 1016 are not paged (pre-flight I3; backlog). The page name is `"Page {page + 1}"`. Values and their text are the MCU's: the heard value, and `format!("{plain:.2}")`.
     - The view still carries `usize`s; the codec clamps them (Ruling 8: every SysEx value and index is sent as at most 127), so a chain of more than 128 plug-ins shows slot 127 for any slot past it.
     - `PluginSlot(i)` sets slot i, page 0. `PluginPage(n)` moves the page by n, and does nothing (uncounted) past either end.
     - With no selected strip, an empty chain, or a plug-in that is not running, `PluginSlot` and `PluginPage` are refused and counted. The view's page is then `None`, and the keyboard shows an empty chain.
     - The page is computed only when a due Kontrol is in Plug-in mode (`Due.page`).
   - **Why:**
     - The owner wants any plug-in to work.
     - The list order is what the plug-in's own generic view shows. Pages the user arranges are a later refinement (backlog).
   - **Cost if wrong:** the page builder (`slot_param_views`) and its test. Kontakt-class plug-ins with more than 1016 parameters show the first 127 pages only.
7. **Ruling (presets; owner: "preset previous/next").**
   - **What:**
     - `PluginInstance` gains `fn presets(&mut self) -> Presets` (`Presets { names: Vec<String>, current: Option<usize> }`, default empty) and `fn load_preset(&mut self, index: usize) -> Result<(), HostError>` (default `Err(HostError::StateRejected("this plug-in has no presets"))`).
     - **AU:**
       - `names` are the factory presets (`kAudioUnitProperty_FactoryPresets`, a +1 `CFArray` of `AUPreset`; a unit that answers an error has none). Only the **array** is owned (`CFRetained::from_raw` on it). Each entry's `presetName` belongs to the array: it is read borrowed, after a null check, while the array is alive, with the non-owning form the file already uses (`unsafe { &*s }.to_string()`, `au/ffi.rs:526`), never through `cfstring`, which takes ownership and would over-release (pre-flight C2).
       - `current` is the index whose `presetNumber` equals `kAudioUnitProperty_PresentPreset`'s, and `None` for a negative (user) number. The name `PresentPreset`'s get returns is +1 and is released (`cfstring` is right there).
       - `load_preset(i)` sets `PresentPreset` to that entry. After a successful set it reads every watched parameter's value and `HostWrites::note`s it, so the parameter notices the load causes (JUCE-built AUs post one per changed parameter) are taken as the host's echoes in `gestures()`: no "Set …" undo step, no automation lane while a strip writes (pre-flight I2).
     - **CLAP:** keeps the defaults (its presets need the preset-discovery factory; backlog row).
     - **Stepping:** `Preset(±1)` steps the focused plug-in's presets without wrapping. From none, Next loads the first and Previous the last. At an end nothing happens.
     - **Uncounted no-ops:** no presets, or a step to the one loaded. A plug-in that is not running, or a failed load, is refused and counted; the failure adds "Preset: {error}" to the problems.
     - **A load:**
       - It is not an undo step; it is a change made in the plug-in, as when its own window picks a preset.
       - It sets `plugin_dirty` and `news.doc_changed` (the title shows Edited).
       - It adds the slot to `news.params_changed`, so the app re-reads the parameters.
     - The page shows `preset_name` (the loaded name, else ""), `preset` and `presets`.
     - **No preset call per feedback (pre-flight M7).** `SessionSurfaces.presets` caches the focused slot's `Presets` with the time it was read. `surface_page` uses the cache while it is for the same slot and younger than `PRESETS_FRESH` (500 ms), and no `params_changed` named the slot since; otherwise it asks `plugins::presets` again. A load and a slot change refresh it. `surface_preset` (a button press) always asks the plug-in.
     - Under `test-hooks`, `plugins::debug_set_presets(slot, names, current)` makes a slot answer that list, and `debug_current_preset(slot)` reads what was loaded. The core tests use them because the test CLAP plug-ins have no presets.
   - **Why:**
     - These are the plug-in's own presets, with no NKS library and no VizDAW preset store.
     - AU covers the owner's macOS plug-ins.
     - Noting the load's values as host writes is the mechanism that already excuses the host's own parameter writes; it needs no new state.
   - **Cost if wrong:**
     - A reopen is safe: Save (and the bounce) captures each live plug-in's state and keeps only the automated parameters' static values as its overlay (`PluginHost::capture_kept` → `capture_state_keeping`), so a saved preset comes back for every parameter that is not automated, and an automated one keeps its static value, by design (5e-1 Ruling 15), exactly as with the plug-in's own preset menu (pre-flight M1).
     - The residue: `load_preset` bypasses `PluginHost`, so its `Live.base` keeps the pre-preset values. An undo of an older edit of such a parameter restores its pre-preset value, and an automated parameter with no overlay is captured at its pre-preset static value. Picking a preset in the plug-in's own window has the same gap today. Not fixed here: a backlog row ("the host re-reads a plug-in's base values after a preset load").
     - An AU whose notices differ from the values read right after the set (a unit that smooths) can still leave a gesture; README 196 checks Undo and automation.
     - CLAP users see dark preset buttons until the backlog row lands.
8. **Ruling (what the keyboard is sent).**
   - **What:** Everything goes through a cache keyed by message kind and index (CC number; SysEx command and slot). A message is sent only when it differs from the last one with its key, and `refresh()` clears the cache. So does every accepted handshake answer (Ruling 4), and the keyboard's own tempo forgets the tempo entry (Ruling 5).
   - **Seven bits (pre-flight I3):** `sysex` clamps its `value` and `index` to 0x7F ("a value above 127 is sent as 127"); conversions from the view's `usize`s use `u8::try_from(x.min(127))`. Info bytes are 7-bit by construction (`ascii` text, VU levels, tempo bytes).
   - **Per slot k (0–7), showing strip `first + k`:**
     - `40` available: value = the track type. `Instrument` → 2 (MIDI), `Audio` → 3, `Bus` → 5 (return), `Master` → 6, an empty slot → 0.
     - `48` name. `46` volume text: `"{:.1} dB"` of `fader_db`, never `"-0.0"`, and `"-inf dB"` at the floor and whenever the dB is below −99.95 (it would read "-100.0 dB" or longer): at most 8 characters, DrivenByMoss's limit (pre-flight M9). `47` pan text: n is the rounded percentage of `pan_of`; `"C"` whenever n is 0 (never "L0" or "R0", M10), else `"L{n}"` or `"R{n}"`.
     - `42` selected, `43` mute, `44` solo, `45` arm, `4A` muted-by-solo: value 0/1. Muted-by-solo is `view.soloing && !solo`.
     - `4B` colour by kind: Instrument `#3C8CE6`, Audio `#46B45A`, Bus `#E6963C`, Master `#B4B4B4`.
     - CC `50+k` and `58+k`: the volume and pan ring, `round(v × 127)`.
     - Empty slots send type 0, empty text, 0 values and 0 rings.
   - **Once per feedback:**
     - `49` VU: value 2, index 0, 16 bytes, L and R per slot. `vu(peak)` is `round(127 × (dB + 60) / 60)` clamped to 0–127, with 0 for silence, a NaN or a negative peak.
     - `41` track instance: index 0, empty info, on every full refresh. No NI plug-in is handed over (phase 3 is out).
     - `19` tempo, when `view.tempo > 0`: VizDAW's tempo at the transport, always (the keyboard's own change is answered with it, Ruling 5).
     - Transport: `10` = playing, `14` = !playing, `12` = recording, `16` = looping, `11`/`20`/`21` = 1, `13` = `view.count_in` (the record count-in is on), `23` = `view.automation_suspended`, `18` = !recording (Tap works except while recording, Ruling 15), and `17`/`22` = 0 (dark). The lights follow VizDAW's state, whoever changed it (owner, 2026-11-03).
     - Navigation masks: bit 0 = previous possible, bit 1 = next possible.
       - `30` tracks, from the selected index (none selected: next when there are strips).
       - `31` banks in Mixer mode (previous when `first > 0`, next when `first + 8 < strips`), pages in Plug-in mode.
       - `32`/`33` 0.
       - `36` presets (with a page: previous when `preset > 0`, next when `preset + 1 < presets`, both when `preset` is `None` and `presets > 0`; else 0).
     - Selected track: `68` = its type (0 with none shown), `66` mute, `67` solo, `69` muted-by-solo.
     - `05` the mode.
   - **Plug-in mode only, with a page:**
     - `71` chain: each name followed by NUL, then one more NUL. With no page the info is a single NUL.
     - `70` selected slot.
     - `72` names and `73` value texts for k = 0–7, with empty text past the page's parameters. The *value* of `72` is 0 (unipolar) for all.
     - `74` value = pages, index = page.
     - `75` index 0, info = page name.
     - `76` preset name.
     - CC `70+k` rings.
   - **Plug-in mode without a page** (no selected strip, an empty chain, a plug-in not running; pre-flight M5): `71` a single NUL; `70` 0; `72` and `73` empty for k = 0–7; `74` value 0, index 0; `75` ""; `76` ""; CC `70+k` 0; `31` and `36` 0.
   - **Text** is `kontrol::ascii`: printable ASCII kept, any other character `?`, at most `MAX_TEXT` = 32 bytes.
   - **Meters:** `Unit::sent` counts a VU message as a meter, not a log line.
   - **Why:**
     - These are DrivenByMoss's layouts.
     - VizDAW has no track colours yet, so the colour says the kind (backlog: user track colours).
     - DrivenByMoss sends "audio" so that mute and solo work. VizDAW's instrument strips are MIDI tracks, and README 198 checks that mute and solo work on them.
   - **Cost if wrong:** one table entry and its golden test each. The uncertain ones are README 198 checks: 0x75, the 0x72 type, text lengths, VU scale, colour format, 0x41, the mode echo, the nav bits and the Tap, Count-in and Automation lights.
9. **Ruling (the registry's part).**
   - **What:**
     - `Surface` gains `fn shows_page(&self) -> bool { false }` (the Kontrol: Plug-in mode), `fn goodbye(&mut self) -> Vec<Vec<u8>> { Vec::new() }` and `fn take_problem(&mut self) -> Option<String> { None }`.
     - `Registry::remove` answers a unit's holds (`release_all`, owed), then sends a connected, online unit's `goodbye()` while it is still in `units`, then drops it (Ruling 4, I1).
     - `goodbye_all()` sends every connected, online unit's, then quits the registry (Ruling 4, M2).
     - `route` reads `take_problem()` after each input. It also compares `shows_page()` before and after; on a change it sets the unit's `fed` to `None`, so the unit is due at the next service (the same service, for `surface.inject`) with its page (M15).
     - `due` sets `Due.page` when a due unit `shows_page()`. A `PluginParam` moved by another hand makes such a unit due at once, as it does the main MCU.
     - **Unknown kinds (controller ruling K-0, pre-flight C1).** `parse_file` reads `surfaces` as JSON values. An entry whose `"kind"` is a string naming no `SurfaceKind` becomes a `Kept { id, kind, ports, raw }`: its `id` must still be 0–15 and unique across all entries; `ports` are its `"input"` and `"output"` strings when it has them; nothing else in it is checked, so a future kind's extra fields are fine. An entry with a known kind is parsed strictly as today (`Entry`, `deny_unknown_fields`). Anything else that fails (bad JSON, a missing or non-string kind, a known kind with a bad field, a duplicate or out-of-range ID, a port shared with any entry, a command check, more than 16 entries) still sets the file aside.
     - The registry keeps the kept entries (`Registry.kept`): `free_id` skips their IDs; `check_unit` counts them toward `MAX_SURFACES` and refuses their ports ("“X” is used by surface 3"); `file()` writes them back verbatim, in ID order with the rest; each load adds the problem line "surface {id}: “{kind}” needs a newer VizDAW; it is kept in surfaces.json and not driven"; they are not in `surface_list` (no `SurfaceKindRow`), take no port claim, and `surface_remove` answers "there is no surface {id}" for them, as for any ID it does not list.
   - **Why:**
     - This is the smallest change to the 5f-1 registry that keeps MCU and HUI byte for byte. Their trait defaults are the old behaviour.
     - Keeping an unknown entry verbatim, with its ID and ports, is what K-0 needs: a build that only skipped it would drop it at its next save, and a reused ID or port would make the newer build refuse the file ("surface ID appears twice", "a shared port").
   - **Cost if wrong:** a trait method; the kept list and its test.

How Task 1's `Kontrol` behaves. Tasks 2 and 3 extend it.
- `hello()` returns `[[BF 01 04]]` and starts the hello timer at the next feedback.
- `input`:
  - It splits the bytes with `midi::split`; every `Err` is counted.
  - `BF 01 v` is the answer: Ruling 4. Every accepted answer clears the cache, so the next feedback is the identity and the full state; a second answer while online re-greets the same way. An answer below 4 while online takes the unit offline as `BF 02` does and reports the problem once (M13).
  - `BF 02 xx` while online is `set_online(false)` (the releases are answered), and restarts the hello timer: the next hello goes `HELLO_EVERY` after it (the pull-back, Ruling 4).
  - Anything else is ignored and counted. Task 2 replaces this arm.
- `feedback(view, first, now)`:
  - Offline, it is empty, except the hello repeat.
  - Online, it is the identity once after each accepted answer. Task 3 adds the rest.
  - It always records `view` and `first`, so Task 2's input can address strips.
- `goodbye()` returns `[[BF 02 00]]` (Task 3 adds the empty slots before it) whether or not the unit is online, and marks it offline through its `Link` directly: it neither forgets nor answers holds, because the registry answers them first (I1).
- `release_all` returns `Held { touched: vec![], idle: idle.take_all() }` and sets the unit offline. `release_holds` does the same and stays online. `touched()` is empty: the keyboard reports no touch. `refresh()` clears the cache.
- `Kontrol` implements the crate's `Unit` trait (`link`, `idle`, `forget_all`), so going offline answers the releases.

/// handshake and gets its display, rides fader 1 from knob 1 as one "Set

#[test]
#[ignore = "needs a window server and VIZDAW_E2E_APP"]
fn drives_the_mixer_from_a_kontrol_mk3() {
    let mut app = App::start();
    let w = app.call("app.new_demo", json!({}))["window"].clone();
    app.wait(&w, json!({"condition": "documents_at_least", "count": 1}));
    let run = |app: &mut App, id: &str, params: Value| {
        app.call(
            "command.run",
            json!({"id": id, "params": params, "window": w}),
        )
    };
    let port = "KONTROL S88 MK3 DAW";
    run(
        &mut app,
        "surface.add",
        json!({"type": "kontrol_mk3", "input": port, "output": port}),
    );
    let st = app.call("state.get", json!({"target": "surfaces", "window": w}));
    assert_eq!(st["surfaces"][0]["type"], "kontrol_mk3", "{st}");
    assert_eq!(st["surfaces"][0]["online"], false, "{st}");
    let id = st["surfaces"][0]["id"].as_u64().unwrap();
    run(
        &mut app,
        "surface.inject",
        json!({"surface": id, "bytes": "bf 01 04"}),
    );
    let ni = "f0 00 21 09 00 00 44 43 01 00";
    let st = wait_for_state(&mut app, &w, "surfaces", |s| {
        s["feedback"][0]["messages"].as_array().is_some_and(|m| {
            m.iter()
                .any(|x| x.as_str().is_some_and(|t| t.starts_with(ni)))
        })
    });
    assert_eq!(st["surfaces"][0]["online"], true, "{st}");
    let auto = app.call("state.get", json!({"target": "automation", "window": w}));
    let synth = auto["modes"][0]["strip"].as_u64().unwrap();
    run(
        &mut app,
        "surface.inject",
        json!({"surface": id, "bytes": "bf 50 05"}),
    );
    let auto = app.call("state.get", json!({"target": "automation", "window": w}));
    assert_eq!(auto["touched"][0], format!("fader:{synth}"), "{auto}");
    wait_for_state(&mut app, &w, "document", |d| d["undo"] == "Set Fader");
    run(
        &mut app,
        "surface.inject",
        json!({"surface": id, "bytes": "bf 10 01"}),
    );
    app.wait(&w, json!({"condition": "playing"}));
    run(
        &mut app,
        "surface.inject",
        json!({"surface": id, "bytes": "bf 14 01"}),
    );
    app.wait(&w, json!({"condition": "stopped"}));
    // The keyboard's tempo control: 100 BPM is 60 000 000 × 10 ns, sent as
    // 00 0e 4e 1c 00 (Ruling 5).
    run(
        &mut app,
        "surface.inject",
        json!({"surface": id, "bytes": "f0 00 21 09 00 00 44 43 01 00 19 00 00 00 0e 4e 1c 00 f7"}),
    );
    wait_for_state(&mut app, &w, "document", |d| d["undo"] == "Set Tempo");
    let st = app.call("state.get", json!({"target": "surfaces", "window": w}));
    let ignored = st["surfaces"][0]["ignored"].as_u64().unwrap();
    run(
        &mut app,
        "surface.inject",
        json!({"surface": id, "bytes": "f0 00 21 09 00 00 44 43 01 00 48"}),
    );
    let st = app.call("state.get", json!({"target": "surfaces", "window": w}));
    assert_eq!(
        st["surfaces"][0]["ignored"].as_u64(),
        Some(ignored + 1),
        "a cut SysEx is ignored and counted: {st}"
    );
    run(&mut app, "surface.remove", json!({"surface": id}));
    app.quit();
}
